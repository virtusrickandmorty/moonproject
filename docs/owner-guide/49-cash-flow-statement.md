# 49. Cash flow statement

**What it is for:** View where your cash came from and where it went. It groups the money movements into three groups: operating (daily business), investing (buying or selling fixed assets), and financing (loans and owner money).

**Before you start:** You will need to know the date range you want to look at.

### Steps
1. On the **Reports** menu, click **Cash flow statement**.
2. Under **Statement of cash flows**, type the **From** and **To** dates.
3. Click **Show**.

### What the system does for you
It calculates the opening cash you had at the start of your chosen dates. This includes any opening balances you entered when starting the system. Then, under **Direct method — from the posted journals**, it shows the cash that came in and went out during that time. It finishes with the closing cash.

It does not show money moved between your own cash places (like transferring money from the till to the bank), because that does not change your total cash.

At the bottom, it shows a **Check difference** line. This compares the closing cash on the statement with the cash accounts on your balance sheet. This difference must show as zero.

You can save the statement to your computer by clicking **Export CSV**, or print it by clicking **Print**.

### Common mistakes and how to fix them
- **Mistake:** The **Check difference** line does not show zero, and there is a warning message.
- **Fix:** Tell the accountant. This means a manual journal entry was made that broke the cash flow rules. They will need to find and fix the entry.
